SOC Playbook — Phishing Alert Triage

PB-PHISH-001 | Author: Guruprasad S | Version: 1.0 | Classification: INTERNAL

🎯 Why I Wrote This

During my BTL1 training, I realized that when you're new in a SOC, you panic when a phishing alert comes in. You don't know what to check first, what matters, and what can wait. I wrote this playbook for my future self — and any other L1 analyst who needs a checklist when their brain freezes.

Target Audience: L1/L2 SOC Analysts (like me)
SLA: Initial triage within 15 minutes, containment within 1 hour
Priority: P2 (HIGH) — Business hours, P1 (CRITICAL) — After hours if executive targeted

📋 My Step-by-Step Procedure

01

Alert Acknowledgment & Initial Assessment

Acknowledge the alert in the SIEM within 15 minutes. Gather initial context:

  • Alert source (Email Gateway, EDR, User Report) — user reports are often more accurate than automated alerts
  • Recipient email address and department — Finance/HR = higher priority
  • Sender address and subject line — check for urgency words like "IMMEDIATE ACTION REQUIRED"
  • Presence of attachments or links — attachments are worse than links (usually)
02

Email Header Analysis

Extract and analyze email headers to determine true origin. I used to skip this step until I learned how easy spoofing is:

  • Check SPF, DKIM, DMARC alignment — if DMARC fails, be extra suspicious
  • Verify "Return-Path" vs "From" address — mismatch = red flag
  • Inspect X-Originating-IP and Received headers — trace the real path
  • Check for lookalike domains and typosquats — "company-finance.com" vs "companyfinance.com"
$ dig TXT company-finance.com
$ whois 203.78.12.5
$ nslookup -type=MX company-finance.com
03

Attachment & Link Analysis

DO NOT open attachments on production systems. I learned this the hard way in a lab (thankfully it was a VM). Use sandbox:

  • Submit attachment to Any.Run / VirusTotal / Hybrid Analysis — I prefer Any.Run for behavioral analysis
  • Check file hash against threat intelligence feeds — AlienVault OTX, Abuse.ch
  • Analyze URLs with URLScan.io before accessing — never click directly
  • Document file type, size, and creation timestamp — helps with timeline later
04

User Impact Assessment

Determine if the recipient interacted with the email. This is where I used to waste time — now I check logs first, ask user later:

  • Check proxy logs for URL clicks — look for 200 OK responses to suspicious domains
  • Review EDR logs for file execution — check for child processes spawned by Office apps
  • Interview user: "Did you open the attachment?" — be specific, users say "no" when they mean "I closed it immediately"
  • Check mailbox rules or forwarding configured — attackers love setting up forwarding rules
05

Containment Actions

If malicious intent confirmed, execute immediate containment. I prioritize based on blast radius:

  • Delete email from all mailboxes (eDiscovery search) — stops spread to other users
  • Block sender domain at email gateway — prevents follow-up emails
  • Block URLs at proxy/firewall — kills the delivery channel
  • Isolate affected endpoint if file was executed — assume compromise until proven otherwise
06

Documentation & Closure

Complete incident ticket with details. I hate documentation but it's non-negotiable:

  • Full timeline of events — include every timestamp you can find
  • IOCs extracted (IPs, domains, hashes) — format them for easy copy-paste to threat intel
  • Screenshots of analysis results — Any.Run reports, VirusTotal results
  • Actions taken and lessons learned — what worked, what didn't, what to do faster next time

🚨 When to Escalate

Escalate to L3 / Incident Response Team if:

  • Executive or C-level employee targeted (Whaling) — these need immediate attention
  • Multiple users clicked link or opened attachment — indicates successful campaign, not isolated
  • C2 communication detected from internal host — this is no longer just phishing, it's active compromise
  • Ransomware or credential harvester identified — don't try to handle alone as L1
  • Business-critical system compromised — domain controllers, database servers, etc.

📝 Why This Playbook Matters to Me

I wrote this because during my first phishing lab, I spent 45 minutes just figuring out where to start. A playbook gives you structure when you're panicking. It's not perfect — I'm sure senior analysts would add more steps — but it's a solid foundation for a fresher. I'll keep updating it as I learn.
